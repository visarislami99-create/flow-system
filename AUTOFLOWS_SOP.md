# AutoFlows — Client Lifecycle SOP

---

## STAGE 1: IDEAL CLIENT PROFILE (ICP)

### Who You Are Looking For

**Company size:** 100 to 1,000 employees. Sweet spot is 150 to 500.

**Industry:** Any business where employees primarily work on computers and browsers. Best fits:
- SaaS companies
- Professional services (consulting, agencies, law, accounting)
- Fintech and financial services
- Staffing and recruitment firms
- E-commerce operations teams
- Real estate operations
- Insurance

**Decision maker:** CEO or Founder for org-wide buy-in. COO or Head of Operations as a secondary entry point who can escalate internally.

**Revenue range:** $5M to $100M annually. They have budget but are not yet at enterprise procurement complexity.

**Geography:** Start with English-speaking markets. USA, UK, UAE, Australia.

### Buying Signals (What to Look For)

These are indicators a company is a strong fit RIGHT NOW:

1. **Hiring for manual roles.** If they are posting jobs for Operations Coordinator, Sales Ops Manager, Project Coordinator, Customer Success Manager, or similar roles, they are scaling headcount to solve a problem AutoFlows solves with systems. This is the strongest buying signal.

2. **Recent funding.** Series A or B companies. They have capital, are scaling fast, and have ops pain. Find via Crunchbase or LinkedIn funding announcements.

3. **Leadership posts about scaling or AI.** CEOs and COOs who post on LinkedIn about operational challenges, growth bottlenecks, or AI curiosity are warm targets.

4. **High employee count in coordination roles.** If 20 to 40 percent of their LinkedIn headcount is in ops, coordination, account management, or project management, they have a manual work problem.

5. **Tech-forward but no AI deployment.** They use modern tools (HubSpot, Notion, Salesforce, Jira) but have no structured AI layer. They are ready but have not done it yet.

## STAGE 2: LEAD RESEARCH AND SOURCING

### Where to Find Prospects

**LinkedIn Sales Navigator** is the primary tool. Filters to use:
- Company headcount: 100 to 1,000
- Seniority: C-suite, VP, Director
- Title keywords: CEO, Founder, COO, Head of Operations, VP Operations
- Industry: select relevant verticals above
- Geography: USA, UK, UAE, Australia
- Relationship: 2nd degree connections first (warmer, higher reply rate)

**LinkedIn Organic Search**
- Search job titles directly
- Look at who engages on posts about AI, ops efficiency, scaling
- Check followers of relevant influencers in ops and AI space

**Job Boards as a Signal**
- Search LinkedIn Jobs, Indeed, or Greenhouse for companies posting coordination and ops roles
- A company hiring 3+ ops coordinators is a priority target

**Crunchbase / Tracxn**
- Filter companies by funding stage (Series A, B), industry, and location
- Cross-reference founders and COOs on LinkedIn

### How to Research Each Prospect Before Outreach

Before sending any message, spend 5 minutes on each prospect:

1. Read their LinkedIn profile. What do they talk about? What do they care about?
2. Check their company LinkedIn page. What departments do they have? How many employees in ops or coordination roles?
3. Look at recent company posts. Any mention of scaling, hiring, or ops challenges?
4. Note their current tech stack if visible (often listed in job postings).
5. Identify the specific pain point most relevant to their role and company.

This research is what makes the outreach specific and non-generic. Every message should reference something real about their business.

## STAGE 3: OUTREACH SEQUENCE (COLD LINKEDIN DM)

### Step 1 — Connection Request

Send a blank connection request. No message attached.

Reason: connection requests with messages have lower acceptance rates. Get in first, then talk.

### Step 2 — First Message (send 24 to 48 hours after they accept)

Keep it short. One specific observation about their business. One question. Nothing about AutoFlows yet.

Template:

> "Hey [name], thanks for connecting. I noticed [company] has been growing fast on the [ops / GTM / client success] side. Quick question — how is your team handling [specific workflow pain relevant to their role] at the current scale?"

The goal of this message is to start a conversation, not pitch. Ask one question you genuinely want the answer to.

### Step 3 — Second Message (send 5 to 7 days later if no reply)

If they did not respond, send one follow-up. Do not reference the first message. Start fresh.

Template:

> "Hey [name], I am building something I think would be relevant for [company] as you scale the team. Would you be open to a 15-minute conversation this week?"

### Step 4 — Third Message (send 7 days after second if still no reply)

This is the last message. Keep it very short. No pressure.

Template:

> "No worries if the timing is off. I will leave it here — feel free to reach out when it makes sense."

This closes the loop and occasionally triggers a reply from people who were meaning to respond.

### What to Do When They Reply

If they show any interest or ask what you do, respond with a two to three sentence description of AutoFlows and immediately ask for a short call.

Template:

> "We build and deploy an AI dispatch system across the entire business — from C-suite to every computer-based role. Every employee gets parallel AI agents running their tasks. They review outputs instead of doing the work manually. Clicking, thinking, navigation grind, all automated. Given what you described I think there is a real fit. Are you free for 20 minutes this week or next?"

## STAGE 4: PRE-CALL COMMUNICATION

Once they agree to a call, send a calendar invite within one hour. Use Calendly or book directly.

Include in the calendar invite description:

> "Looking forward to connecting. In this call I will learn a bit about how your team currently operates, share what AutoFlows does, and we will both know by the end whether it makes sense to go further. No pressure, no deck. Just a real conversation."

This sets expectations and reduces no-show rate. People are more likely to show when they know what to expect.

## STAGE 5: DISCOVERY CALL (20 to 30 minutes)

### Goal
Understand their business deeply enough to identify which roles have the highest automation ROI. Do not pitch yet. Listen.

### Agenda

**Minutes 0 to 5 — Context setting**

Open with:

> "Before I tell you anything about AutoFlows, I want to understand how your team actually works. That way I can tell you honestly if this is a fit or not. Is that okay?"

This disarms the sales dynamic immediately.

**Minutes 5 to 20 — Discovery questions**

Ask these in natural conversation, not as a checklist:

- How many people are on your team total, and how is it structured?
- Walk me through what a typical day looks like for your [ops / account management / coordinator] team.
- Where does work get stuck or slow down most often?
- How many of your roles are primarily computer and browser based?
- Have you rolled out any AI tools before? What happened?
- If you could remove one type of work from your team's plate tomorrow, what would it be?
- Who in the company would need to sign off on something like this?

**Minutes 20 to 25 — Brief AutoFlows overview**

Now you describe the product. Keep it to 3 to 5 minutes. Plain language. No jargon.

> "What we do is build an AI operating system across your entire business. Every employee with a computer-based role gets parallel AI agents running their tasks in the background. They review the outputs and approve them instead of doing the work manually. We set it up, we deploy it, and we train your whole team to use it. The whole company runs faster without adding headcount."

**Minutes 25 to 30 — Close for next step**

> "Based on what you told me, I can put together a quick ROI breakdown showing which roles make the most sense to start with and what the expected output looks like. Would it be worth getting back on a call next week so I can walk you through it?"

### What Visar Does Manually After This Call

- Write up a brief summary of what you learned about the company (roles, pain points, tech stack, decision makers)
- Identify the top 3 to 5 roles with the highest automation ROI
- Estimate time saved per week per role
- Calculate rough annual value (time saved x hourly cost x headcount in that role)
- Prepare a one-page ROI brief for the sales call

## STAGE 6: SALES CALL (30 to 45 minutes)

### Goal
Present the ROI brief, walk through what AutoFlows looks like deployed in their specific company, agree on scope and pricing, and close.

### Agenda

**Minutes 0 to 5 — Recap**

> "Last time you told me [brief recap of their main pain points]. I went away and mapped out which roles in your company have the highest automation ROI. Can I walk you through what I found?"

**Minutes 5 to 20 — ROI brief presentation**

Walk through the brief verbally. Cover:
- Top 3 to 5 roles you are recommending to start with
- What the workflow looks like before and after AutoFlows for each role
- Time saved per week per role
- Annual value estimate
- What the employee experience looks like (parallel agents, review loop, approval workflow)

Be specific to their company. Use the names of their actual roles and real examples from the discovery call.

**Minutes 20 to 30 — Address objections**

Common objections and how to handle them:

*"We tried AI tools before and adoption was low."*
> "That is exactly why we do not give your team a tool to experiment with. We replace the workflow entirely and train the team to use it. The path of least resistance becomes using the system, not going around it."

*"How long does it take to set up?"*
> "Discovery and build takes two to four weeks. Deployment and training takes another one to two weeks. You are running within six weeks."

*"What does it cost?"*
> "The build and deployment is a fixed project fee. Depending on company size and the number of roles in scope, that runs between three and eight thousand dollars. There is an optional monthly retainer for ongoing optimization and new department rollouts."

*"I need to loop in someone else."*
> "Of course. Who else needs to be part of this conversation? I am happy to do this same walkthrough with them or put together a document they can review."

**Minutes 30 to 45 — Close**

> "Based on what we covered today, do you want to move forward? The next step is the discovery engagement where I get into the detail of your workflows and build the context file. I can send over a simple one-page agreement today and we can start next week."

If they are not ready to close on the call:

> "What would need to happen for you to feel confident moving forward?"

Listen to the answer. That tells you exactly what the real objection is.

### What Visar Does Manually After This Call

- Send the one-page agreement within one hour of the call
- Follow up if no response within 48 hours
- If they ghost, send one final message after 5 days and then move on

## STAGE 7: POST-SALE ONBOARDING PROCESS

### Phase 1 — Discovery and Context Build (2 to 4 weeks)

This is where Visar does the strategic consulting work. The output is the master business context file.

**Week 1: Business audit**
- Interview the CEO or founder: company structure, strategic goals, what they want the business to look like in 12 months
- Interview each department head: what does their team do, what is the daily workflow, where is the friction, what tools do they use
- Document every computer-based role in the business and its core tasks

**Week 2 to 3: Context file build**
- Write the master business context file. This is the document that tells the AI system how the entire business runs.
- It contains: company overview, department structure, role descriptions, workflow maps, tools used, communication standards, compliance requirements, and how decisions get made
- This file is installed into every employee's Claude setup as the foundation

**Week 4: Review and sign-off**
- Present the context file to the CEO and department heads
- Make adjustments based on their feedback
- Final sign-off before deployment

### Phase 2 — Deployment (1 to 2 weeks)

- Install the context file across the business
- Run the employee interview wizard with each team member or department in sequence
- The wizard interviews each employee, learns their specific role, tasks, and working style, and configures their personal AI setup
- Configure parallel dispatch agents for each role's core task types
- Test with one employee per department before full rollout

**What Visar does manually here:**
- Oversee the wizard installation for each department
- Troubleshoot any configuration issues
- Make real-time adjustments to the context file based on what the wizard surfaces

### Phase 3 — Training (1 to 2 weeks)

- Run training sessions per department (not company-wide — department-by-department is more effective)
- Teach employees how to command the AI, how to review outputs, how to approve and use the completed work
- Train managers on how to monitor their team's AI usage
- Leave each department with a one-page reference guide specific to their role

**What Visar does manually here:**
- Run the training sessions live (video call or in-person for large clients)
- Answer edge case questions
- Collect feedback and make context file adjustments

### Phase 4 — Go-Live and First 30 Days

- Team is live on the system
- Visar monitors usage in week one and checks in daily with department heads
- Weekly check-in call with the CEO or COO for the first month
- Fix any issues that arise in real usage immediately

### Phase 5 — Retainer (Ongoing, Optional)

Monthly retainer covers:
- Ongoing optimization of the context file as the business evolves
- New department or role onboarding as the company grows or hires
- Monthly performance review with the CEO or COO
- Prompt updates when AI capabilities improve
- Priority support for any system issues

## CLIENT SUCCESS METRIC

The client is succeeding when:
- Employees are completing their core daily tasks using AI outputs rather than doing them manually
- The CEO or COO can see measurable throughput increase without adding headcount
- The team is asking to expand AutoFlows to new roles or departments (this is the strongest signal)

When expansion conversations start, move them to a new scope proposal. That is a new project fee.
